### Proposed Term Paper Title
**"The Future of Industrial Engineering in the Generative AI Era: Autonomous Operations, Human-in-the-Loop Synergies, and Next-Generation Competency Paradigms"**

---

### Project Scope & Executive Objectives
This term paper investigates the transformative convergence of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Generative AI (GenAI)—including Large Language Models (LLMs), multimodal foundation models, and synthetic data engines—with classic Industrial Engineering (IE) domains. By synthesizing recent scientific literature from ScienceDirect, IEEE Xplore, and IISE/INFORMS repositories, the research addresses the professor's five core thematic inquiries:

1. **AI vs. GenAI Foundations & Workplace Disruption**: Distinguishing classical predictive/prescriptive AI (e.g., MILP, CNNs) from generative paradigms (diffusion, transformer-based agents) and analyzing their labor-capital productivity impacts on discrete and continuous manufacturing.
2. **IE Sub-Disciplines Automation Assessment**: Evaluating the susceptibility of production planning, facility layout design, line balancing, discrete-event simulation, inventory forecasting, and statistical quality control (SQC) to full vs. partial automation.
3. **Failure Modes, Hallucinations & Deterministic Constraints**: Characterizing critical operational vulnerabilities of GenAI—such as probabilistic hallucinations, lack of physical conservation grounding, data leakage, and unexplainable "black-box" decision outputs in high-stakes manufacturing environments.
4. **The Evolving Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Role**: Redefining the human Industrial Engineer from a manual calculator/scheduler into a high-level systems architect, ethical governor, prompt engineer, and cyber-physical integrator.
5. **Future-Proof Graduate Competencies**: Constructing a high-yield curriculum and skills blueprint (Python, Operations Research, prompt validation, digital twins, change management) to guarantee long-term competitive advantage.
