## Question 1: Electron Configurations & Periodic Classification

### Problem Statement
Determine whether each of the electron configurations given below is an **inert gas (Group 0)**, a **halogen (Group VIIA)**, an **alkali metal (Group IA)**, an **alkaline earth metal (Group IIA)**, or a **transition metal (Groups IIIB & IIB)**. Justify your choices.

* **(a)** $1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^2 3p^6 3d^7 4s^2$
* **(b)** $1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^2 3p^6$
* **(c)** $1s^2 2s^2 2p^5$
* **(d)** $1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^2$
* **(e)** $1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^2 3p^6 3d^2 4s^2$
* **(f)** $1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^2 3p^6 4s^1$

---

### Step-by-Step Solutions & Justifications

#### (a) $1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^2 3p^6 3d^7 4s^2$
* **Total Electrons**: $2 + 2 + 6 + 2 + 6 + 7 + 2 = 27$ electrons ($Z = 27$, Cobalt, $\text{Co}$).
* **Analysis**: The outermost shell is $n=4$ with two $4s$ electrons, while the inner $3d$ subshell is partially filled ($3d^7$, holding 7 out of a maximum 10 electrons).
* **Classification**: **Transition Metal (Group VIIIB / d-block)**.
* **Justification**: Elements with partially filled inner $d$-subshells are by definition transition elements.

#### (b) $1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^2 3p^6$
* **Total Electrons**: $2 + 2 + 6 + 2 + 6 = 18$ electrons ($Z = 18$, Argon, $\text{Ar}$).
* **Analysis**: The outermost principal shell ($n=3$) has full $s$ and $p$ subshells ($3s^2 3p^6$), comprising a complete stable octet of 8 valence electrons.
* **Classification**: **Inert Gas (Group 0 / Group VIIIA / Noble Gas)**.
* **Justification**: A completely filled $s^2 p^6$ valence shell has no unpaired electrons and exceptionally high ionization energy, making it chemically unreactive.

#### (c) $1s^2 2s^2 2p^5$
* **Total Electrons**: $2 + 2 + 5 = 9$ electrons ($Z = 9$, Fluorine, $\text{F}$).
* **Analysis**: The outermost principal shell ($n=2$) contains 7 valence electrons ($2s^2 2p^5$), exactly one electron short of a closed octet ($2p^6$).
* **Classification**: **Halogen (Group VIIA)**.
* **Justification**: Elements possessing an $ns^2 np^5$ valence structure aggressively seek one additional electron to form a stable $-1$ anion.

#### (d) $1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^2$
* **Total Electrons**: $2 + 2 + 6 + 2 = 12$ electrons ($Z = 12$, Magnesium, $\text{Mg}$).
* **Analysis**: The outermost shell is $n=3$, possessing two valence electrons in the $s$ subshell ($3s^2$).
* **Classification**: **Alkaline Earth Metal (Group IIA)**.
* **Justification**: Elements with an $ns^2$ valence configuration outside a noble gas core readily lose their two outer $s$-electrons to form $+2$ cations.

#### (e) $1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^2 3p^6 3d^2 4s^2$
* **Total Electrons**: $2 + 2 + 6 + 2 + 6 + 2 + 2 = 22$ electrons ($Z = 22$, Titanium, $\text{Ti}$).
* **Analysis**: Possesses outer $4s^2$ electrons alongside an incompletely filled inner $3d$ subshell ($3d^2$).
* **Classification**: **Transition Metal (Group IVB / d-block)**.
* **Justification**: Contains an unfilled inner $d$-orbital ($d^2$), characteristic of transition metals.

#### (f) $1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^2 3p^6 4s^1$
* **Total Electrons**: $2 + 2 + 6 + 2 + 6 + 1 = 19$ electrons ($Z = 19$, Potassium, $\text{K}$).
* **Analysis**: Possesses a single valence electron in the outermost $4s$ subshell ($4s^1$) outside a stable argon core.
* **Classification**: **Alkali Metal (Group IA)**.
* **Justification**: Elements with an $ns^1$ valence structure have exceptionally low first ionization energies and form $+1$ cations.

## Question 2: Bonding Differences & Percent Ionic Character

### Part I: Fundamental Differences Among Primary Bonds
1. **Ionic Bonding**:
   * *Mechanism*: Mutual electrostatic attraction between positively charged metal cations and negatively charged nonmetal anions following complete electron transfer.
   * *Directionality*: **Non-directional** (Coulombic fields act uniformly in all spherical directions).
   * *Characteristics*: High melting points, brittle, electrical insulators as solids, conductive when molten.
2. **Covalent Bonding**:
   * *Mechanism*: Localized sharing of valence electron pairs between adjacent atoms of similar electronegativities to fill atomic orbitals.
   * *Directionality*: **Highly directional** (forms along specific hybrid orbital angles, e.g., $109.5^\circ$ for $sp^3$).
   * *Characteristics*: High hardness, directional cleavage, wide range of electrical conductivity.
3. **Metallic Bonding**:
   * *Mechanism*: Positive ion cores held together by electrostatic attraction to a shared, delocalized "sea of mobile conduction electrons."
   * *Directionality*: **Non-directional** (electron cloud flows freely around ion cores).
   * *Characteristics*: High electrical and thermal conductivity, excellent ductility and plastic malleability.

## Question 3: $K^+ - Cl^-$ Potential Energy Curve & Equilibrium State

### Problem Statement
For a $K^+ - Cl^-$ ion pair, attractive and repulsive energies depend on interionic distance $r$ according to:
$$E_A(r) = -\frac{1.436}{r} \qquad E_R(r) = \frac{5.8 \times 10^{-6}}{r^9}$$
Where energies are in $\text{eV}$ and $r$ is in nanometers ($\text{nm}$).
The net potential energy is $E_N(r) = E_A(r) + E_R(r)$.

* **(a)** Calculate the equilibrium interionic spacing ($r_0$).
* **(b)** Calculate the bonding energy ($E_0$).

---

### Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

#### Part (a): Equilibrium Spacing $r_0$
At equilibrium, the net interatomic force is zero, meaning the net potential energy curve reaches its minimum:
$$\left.\frac{dE_N}{dr}\right|_{r = r_0} = 0$$

Given:
$$E_N(r) = -1.436 \, r^{-1} + 5.8 \times 10^{-6} \, r^{-9}$$

Differentiating with respect to $r$:
$$\frac{dE_N}{dr} = (-1.436)(-1) r^{-2} + (5.8 \times 10^{-6})(-9) r^{-10} = \frac{1.436}{r^2} - \frac{5.22 \times 10^{-5}}{r^{10}}$$

Setting the derivative to zero at $r = r_0$:
$$\frac{1.436}{r_0^2} = \frac{5.22 \times 10^{-5}}{r_0^{10}}$$

Cross-multiplying:
$$1.436 \, r_0^8 = 5.22 \times 10^{-5} \implies r_0^8 = \frac{5.22 \times 10^{-5}}{1.436} = 3.6351 \times 10^{-5}$$

Taking the 8th root:
$$r_0 = \left( 3.6351 \times 10^{-5} \right)^{\frac{1}{8}} = \mathbf{0.2789\text{ nm}} \approx \mathbf{0.279\text{ nm}} \quad (2.79\text{ \AA})$$

---

#### Part (b): Bonding Energy $E_0$
Substitute $r_0 = 0.2789\text{ nm}$ into $E_N(r)$:
$$E_0 = E_N(r_0) = -\frac{1.436}{0.2789} + \frac{5.8 \times 10^{-6}}{(0.2789)^9}$$

Evaluating each term:
* Attractive Energy: $E_A = -\frac{1.436}{0.2789} = -5.1488\text{ eV}$
* Repulsive Energy: $E_R = \frac{5.8 \times 10^{-6}}{(0.2789)^9} = \frac{5.8 \times 10^{-6}}{1.0138 \times 10^{-5}} = +0.5721\text{ eV}$

Summing the two terms:
$$E_0 = -5.1488 + 0.5721 = \mathbf{-4.577\text{ eV}} \approx \mathbf{-4.58\text{ eV}}$$

*(Alternative algebraic check: $E_0 = -\frac{A}{r_0}(1 - 1/n) = -\frac{1.436}{0.2789}\left(1 - \frac{1}{9}\right) = -5.1488 \times \frac{8}{9} = -4.577\text{ eV}$. Perfect match!)*

## Question 4: Electrostatic Force Balance Between $K^+$ and $O^{2-}$

### Problem Statement
* **(a)** Calculate the force of attraction using Coulomb's law between a $K^+$ ion ($z_1 = +1$) and an $O^{2-}$ ion ($z_2 = -2$) separated by $r = 1.5\text{ nm}$.
* **(b)** Calculate the repulsive force if $r = 1.5\text{ nm}$ is the equilibrium interatomic spacing.

---

### Step-by-Step Solution

#### Part (a): Force of Attraction ($F_A$)
Coulomb's Law for interionic attractive force:
$$F_A = \frac{|z_1| |z_2| e^2}{4\pi \varepsilon_0 r^2}$$

Physical Constants:
* Electron charge: $e = 1.602 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}$
* Permittivity of free space: $\varepsilon_0 = 8.854 \times 10^{-12}\text{ C}^2/(\text{N}\cdot\text{m}^2) \text{ [or F/m]}$
* Valences: $|z_1| = 1$ ($K^+$), $|z_2| = 2$ ($O^{2-}$) $\implies |z_1 z_2| = 2$
* Distance: $r = 1.5\text{ nm} = 1.5 \times 10^{-9}\text{ m}$

Substitute values into the equation:
$$F_A = \frac{2 \times (1.602 \times 10^{-19})^2}{4 \pi \times (8.854 \times 10^{-12}) \times (1.5 \times 10^{-9})^2}$$

Calculate numerator:
$$\text{Numerator} = 2 \times 2.5664 \times 10^{-38} = 5.1328 \times 10^{-38}\text{ C}^2$$

Calculate denominator:
$$\text{Denominator} = 4 \pi \times 8.854 \times 10^{-12} \times 2.25 \times 10^{-18} = 1.11265 \times 10^{-10} \times 2.25 \times 10^{-18} = 2.5035 \times 10^{-28}$$

Divide:
$$F_A = \frac{5.1328 \times 10^{-38}}{2.5035 \times 10^{-28}} = \mathbf{2.05 \times 10^{-10}\text{ N}} \quad (\mathbf{0.205\text{ nN}})$$

---

#### Part (b): Repulsive Force at Equilibrium ($F_R$)
By definition, at the equilibrium spacing $r_0 = 1.5\text{ nm}$, the net interatomic force must be zero:
$$F_N = F_A + F_R = 0 \implies F_R = -F_A$$

Since the attractive force acts inward with magnitude $2.05 \times 10^{-10}\text{ N}$, the repulsive force must act outward with equal magnitude:
$$F_R = \mathbf{+2.05 \times 10^{-10}\text{ N}} \quad (\mathbf{+0.205\text{ nN}})$$

## Question 5: Macroscopic to Microscopic Stoichiometric Calculations

### Part (a): Gold Wire Atom Count
**Problem**: Calculate the number of atoms in a gold wire that is $0.70\text{ mm}$ in diameter and $8.0\text{ cm}$ in length. The density of gold is $\rho = 19.3\text{ g/cm}^3$.

#### Step 1: Geometric Volume Calculation
* Diameter: $d = 0.70\text{ mm} = 0.070\text{ cm}$
* Length: $L = 8.0\text{ cm}$
* Cross-sectional area:
  $$A_{cross} = \frac{\pi}{4} d^2 = \frac{\pi}{4} (0.070\text{ cm})^2 = 3.8485 \times 10^{-3}\text{ cm}^2$$
* Total volume:
  $$V = A_{cross} \times L = 3.8485 \times 10^{-3} \times 8.0 = 0.030788\text{ cm}^3$$

#### Step 2: Mass Calculation
$$m = \rho \times V = 19.3\text{ g/cm}^3 \times 0.030788\text{ cm}^3 = 0.5942\text{ g}$$

#### Step 3: Moles of Gold
From the periodic table, atomic weight of Gold ($\text{Au}$): $A_{Au} = 196.97\text{ g/mol}$.
$$n_{mol} = \frac{m}{A_{Au}} = \frac{0.5942\text{ g}}{196.97\text{ g/mol}} = 3.0167 \times 10^{-3}\text{ mol}$$

#### Step 4: Total Number of Atoms
Using Avogadro's number ($N_A = 6.022 \times 10^{23}\text{ atoms/mol}$):
$$N = n_{mol} \times N_A = 3.0167 \times 10^{-3} \times 6.022 \times 10^{23} = \mathbf{1.82 \times 10^{21}\text{ atoms}}$$

---

### Part (b): Number of Atoms in One Ton of Iron
**Problem**: Calculate the number of atoms in one ton of iron ($\text{Fe}$). The atomic weight of iron is $A_{Fe} = 55.85\text{ g/mol}$.

#### 1. Metric Ton Calculation ($1\text{ metric ton} = 1000\text{ kg} = 10^6\text{ g}$):
$$m = 1.0 \times 10^6\text{ g}$$
$$n_{mol} = \frac{m}{A_{Fe}} = \frac{1.0 \times 10^6\text{ g}}{55.85\text{ g/mol}} = 17,905.1\text{ mol}$$
$$N = n_{mol} \times N_A = 17,905.1 \times 6.022 \times 10^{23} = \mathbf{1.08 \times 10^{28}\text{ atoms}}$$

#### 2. US Short Ton Calculation ($1\text{ short ton} = 2000\text{ lb} = 907.185\text{ kg} = 9.07185 \times 10^5\text{ g}$):
$$n_{mol} = \frac{9.07185 \times 10^5\text{ g}}{55.85\text{ g/mol}} = 16,243.2\text{ mol}$$
$$N = 16,243.2 \times 6.022 \times 10^{23} = \mathbf{9.78 \times 10^{27}\text{ atoms}}$$
